In 2000, the J-SH04 was sold in Japan. It had a back-facing built-in camera with a 110,000-pixl CMOS imaging sensor.. It could instantly transmit photos via carrier network. It is considered the first mass-market camera phone. Sharp initally offered the phone to NTT DoCoMo, Japan's biggest mobile network operator, but they rejected it. Their second offer, KDDI, also turned it down. When Sharp approached J-Phone, the smallest operator, their engineers thought it was a good idea, but the marketing team didn't agree. They compromised: J-Phone ordereed 10,000 units, but only 2,000 of them were the camera variant.

The camera variant sold out within two weeks while the non-camera variant remained on shelves. J-Phone's Sha-mail picture messagin service quickly became a international reference for the potential of picture sharing.
